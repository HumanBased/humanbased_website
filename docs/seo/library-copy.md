## New pages (from Luis's founding document, light trims)

### know-thyself
- Quote: "Know thyself." Dating phrased as "according to ancient writers such as Plato and Pausanias" (no century).
- Sources checked: Plato, Protagoras 343a-b (Seven Sages dedicate "Know thyself" at Delphi); Pausanias 10.24.1 (Jones and Ormerod translation: sages dedicated the maxims in the fore-temple). Wikipedia "Know thyself" for the fifth-century BC dating.
- Apology: Jowett translation, "the life which is unexamined is not worth living", and the mission of "searching into myself and other men" (paraphrased on the page, no quotation marks).
- Added sentences: Seven Sages and fifth-century note; Socrates' mission put his life at risk; the Know-thyself reading paragraph (phrased as aims).

### anamnesis
- Luis's quote "Learning is nothing but recollection" could not be matched in the Meno. The page uses Jowett's Meno (81d) wording instead: "All enquiry and all learning is but recollection." (first word capitalised from "for all enquiry...").
- Meno slave boy and square: described in general terms. Cave: Republic Book VII.
- The "convergence of independent frameworks" sentence is not used. "Nine frameworks" is not used.

### exile-and-return
- Rumi line: opening of "Out Beyond Ideas of Wrongdoing and Rightdoing", The Essential Rumi (HarperCollins, 1995), credited to Coleman Barks and John Moyne. Page says "in the widely quoted translation by Coleman Barks".
- Masnavi opening with the reed: general knowledge, not quoted.
- Coach and mentorship sentences are phrased as what the app aims to do.

## Written pages (350 to 500 words each)

### thelema
- Quote: "Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law", Liber AL vel Legis I:40 (confirmed via osogd.org).
- Facts: Book of the Law written in Cairo, April 1904 (Wikipedia, Thelema and Book of the Law articles); True Will as a calling kept apart from the ego's ordinary wants (Wikipedia, Thelema); every man and woman a star (paraphrased); Rabelais' Abbey of Thélème and "fay ce que vouldras" (Wikipedia).
- Crowley's dates 1875 to 1947: general knowledge. No endorsement of occult practice; he is flagged as controversial.

### tao
- Quote: Tao Te Ching ch. 37, Legge 1891 (sacred-texts.com). Ch. 8 (water) and ch. 64 (thousand li) are paraphrased, Legge wording checked on the same site.
- Facts: 81 chapters, Laozi attribution debated, Guodian slips around 300 BC (Wikipedia, Tao Te Ching); wu wei as not-doing (Wikipedia, Wu wei).
- Zhuangzi: Zhuang Zhou c. 369 BC to between 301 and 286 BC (Wikipedia); Cook Ding in chapter 3, "Nourishing the Lord of Life". The knife-sharpness detail is from the story itself and was not re-checked against a translation.

### eudaimonia
- Quote: Rackham translation, Nicomachean Ethics I.7 1098a, as cited on Wikipedia's Nicomachean Ethics article.
- Facts: eu plus daimon, "flourishing" as a translation, activity not mere disposition, external goods, pleasure as a by-product, virtues formed by habit (Wikipedia, Eudaimonia and Nicomachean Ethics). "One swallow does not make a spring" is paraphrased.

### magnum-opus
- No pull quote: no verified quotation was found, so none is used.
- Facts: four stages and the note that sources differ, no single recipe (Wikipedia, Magnum opus (alchemy)); Jung, Psychology and Alchemy, individuation (general knowledge plus Wikipedia).

### logotherapy
- Quote: "He who has a why to live for can bear with almost any how", Frankl quoting Nietzsche in Man's Search for Meaning (text from a course PDF at colorado.edu; edition not identified, so the wording may differ slightly between translations).
- The "last of the human freedoms" line contains an em dash in the source, so it is paraphrased, not quoted. The "meaning differs from man to man" line is paraphrased.
- Facts: 1946 German title, English title history, three sources of meaning (Wikipedia). Frankl's dates and camps: general knowledge. "Will to meaning": Frankl's term, not found in the book text checked, so the page does not attribute it to a specific passage.
